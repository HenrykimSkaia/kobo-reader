The Last Lantern of Greyhaven

Chapter 7: The Road Beneath the World

Volume I — The City That Forgot the Sky

When a city tilts, rich people discover gravity at the same speed as everyone else.

Carriages slid through market squares. Statues leaned from their pedestals. Furniture burst from windows and joined the western migration. Across Greyhaven, thousands of citizens clung to railings while the six remaining chains screamed beneath the streets.

From the shattered archive, Rowan watched the dark seventh tower sink lower than the others.

The star-map showed the broken chain dragging through the Gloam. One by one, the six locks beneath the city turned.

Headmistress Rook stood amid fallen papers, blood running from a cut above her eye.

“You knew,” Rowan said.

She looked at the map in his hands.

“I knew the official history was incomplete.”

“That is an elegant phrase for a lie.”

“It is the phrase institutions use.”

Silas sat against the wall while a healer wrapped his ribs. He still held the map he had drawn inside the Blackwing: blue water, green islands, a white sun. Rook refused to look at it.

Mira noticed.

“Why is the sky forbidden?” she asked.

The headmistress removed her silver spectacles.

“Because Greyhaven’s founders came from below.”

Even the clocks seemed to listen.

Rowan thought of the masked figure.

A door that learned to knock.

“And my mother?”

“Elian discovered the old maps. She believed the locks were failing because they had been designed to open from both sides. She went below to learn who was knocking.”

“You let her go.”

“I tried to stop her.”

“Did you expel her a third time?”

Rook almost smiled.

“No. She expelled herself.”

The clock tower lurched again. A crack ran through the ceiling.

Master Fen shouted from the gallery that the western support bridges had begun to collapse.

Rook turned to the assembled Lamplighters.

“We must relight the seventh tower.”

Silas forced himself upright. “The lantern heart is beneath the broken chain. No ordinary route reaches it now.”

The star rose from Rowan’s hand.

A road of blue light unfolded through the open wall, descending into the Gloam.

Everyone looked at him.

He disliked this immediately.

“No,” Silas said.

Rook’s voice was quiet. “He is the only one who can see the unroad.”

“He is fourteen.”

“Fourteen and six months,” Rowan said.

Silas rounded on him. “This is not the moment to become precise.”

“It is always the moment to become precise. You taught me that.”

“I regret teaching you almost everything.”

The city tilted farther.

Somewhere below, a lock opened with a sound like a mountain exhaling.

Rowan fastened the star beneath his coat.

“I am going.”

Mira stepped beside him.

“So am I.”

Silas opened his mouth.

She gave him a look that closed it.

Pip strapped the memory anchor across his chest. The device now had three copper handgrips and an alarming number of fresh wires.

“I should come because I built the only machine capable of keeping us ourselves.”

“You should stay because you built it,” Mira said.

“Both arguments are excellent. I choose the one with danger.”

Rook gave them three flame staffs and a glass vessel containing the academy’s oldest memory: the first lighting of Greyhaven. It glowed gold inside the bottle.

“This will restart the lantern heart,” she said.

Rowan held it to the light.

Inside, faceless founders raised seven flames while a city formed around them.

Something about the scene felt wrong.

The descent began from the clock tower’s broken western balcony.

Rowan stepped onto empty air.

The unroad appeared beneath his boot, a narrow line of blue light curving into the mist. Mira followed without hesitation. Pip followed while breathing in a way that suggested he had recently remembered every sensible decision he had ever ignored.

Silas remained on the balcony.

“Rowan!”

Rowan looked back.

Silas held up the unfinished map.

“Return,” he said.

Not be brave.

Not save the city.

Return.

Rowan nodded.

Then the Gloam swallowed the tower.

The unroad wound beneath Greyhaven. Above them hung the city’s underside: forests of pipes, roots from suspended gardens, ancient foundations, and six chains thick enough to carry houses. The broken seventh chain swung through the mist, each movement producing a deep metallic groan.

They fought their way downward.

The Gloamers reached for names, memories, certainty. Pip’s anchor rang whenever one came close, sending their identities through the wire.

Rowan Vale: mapmaker, stubborn, frightened, moving anyway.

Mira Quill: watchful, furious, loyal by choice.

Pippin Noll: inventor, liar about safety, teller of truth through jokes.

Each name became armour.

At the end of the road floated the seventh lantern heart.

It was a cathedral-sized sphere of black glass suspended over the void. The broken chain had torn through its side. Inside, the ancient flame had collapsed to a single red coal.

They entered through the wound.

The interior held thousands of glass threads. Each contained a memory taken from the city: first steps, weddings, recipes, faces, funerals, names of vanished streets. Greyhaven had not merely forgotten its history.

The lanterns had been burning it.

Mira touched one thread and recoiled.

“The city’s light is made from memory.”

Rowan looked at the golden bottle Rook had given him.

The founders’ first lighting.

A memory prepared for sacrifice.

“No,” he said.

The coal dimmed.

Above them, another lock began to turn.

“We need a flame,” Mira said.

“We need a memory heavy enough to burn.”

Pip lifted the anchor. “What if it does not burn?”

Rowan understood.

“A memory can be lost because nobody carries it,” he said. “Or it can become stronger because several people do.”

“That is a beautiful theory,” Mira said.

“It is also the only one we have.”

“Then it is an excellent theory.”

They placed their hands on the copper grips.

Pip turned the handle.

The device hummed.

“Choose something recent,” he warned. “I have not tested deep childhood and prefer not to discover that one of you was secretly a goat.”

Rowan closed his eyes.

He chose the moment in the dormitory when Pip’s ridiculous machine first worked and Mira guarded the window. Fear had filled the room. Silas was missing. The city was ending.

And yet Rowan had laughed.

Mira chose the same moment from her side: Rowan laughing despite himself, the first proof that grief had not taken everything.

Pip chose the sight of both of them laughing at him, which he considered one of his greatest technical successes.

The anchor released a clear, ringing note.

Blue-gold fire poured into the lantern heart.

The coal ignited.

Light raced along the glass threads. Memories flashed through the sphere, not consumed but illuminated. The broken chain lifted. Across the Gloam, the six turning locks slammed shut.

Greyhaven steadied.

Far above, the seventh lantern tower blazed back to life.

For several seconds, Rowan felt only relief.

Then the compass opened.

Its needle spun past HOME, TRUTH, and NAME.

It pointed to SKY.

Down.

The Gloam beneath the lantern heart began to clear.

Not all at once. A circle opened in the mist, widening until Rowan saw what lay below.

Blue.

An impossible blue without walls or bridges.

White clouds drifted beneath their feet. Far below the clouds shone an ocean bright with sunlight. Green islands moved across it, carried on the backs of creatures so vast that mountains grew along their shells.

Mira forgot to speak.

Pip whispered, “I may need a larger map.”

A voice came from the compass.

Faint. Broken by distance.

Rowan knew it before it formed his name.

“Ro.”

His mother.

He gripped the case with both hands.

“Where are you?”

Static hissed. Wind. Then Elian’s voice returned.

“Below. Listen carefully. The seventh lantern is not a shield.”

Across the blue world, tiny lights appeared.

One.

Ten.

Hundreds.

Lanterns answering Greyhaven’s flame.

“It is a beacon,” Elian said.

Something moved between the sunlit islands.

Something that had been waiting for the city to announce itself.

The compass needle trembled.

“Find me before they find you.”

The connection died.

Above Rowan, the relit lantern burned brighter.

And all across the world below, the answering lights began to rise.
