The Last Lantern of Greyhaven

Chapter 6: The Messenger Made of Night

Volume I — The City That Forgot the Sky

The Blackwing entered the academy without breaking the wall.

It persuaded the wall that it had never existed.

Stone faded into grey mist. Shelves folded into darkness. The archive floor tipped toward the open air, and Rowan slid among a storm of loose pages while the creature’s enormous beak forced its way through the tower.

Mira caught a brass railing with one hand and Rowan’s coat with the other.

Pip sailed past them seated in a filing drawer.

“I have found transport!”

“You have found furniture!” Mira shouted.

“In a crisis, categories become flexible!”

Headmistress Rook appeared on the upper gallery with six Lamplighters behind her. They drove their staffs into the floor.

“Name the tower!” she commanded.

Blue fire leapt from staff to staff.

Students shouted from the halls.

“Stone!”

“Clock!”

“Shelter!”

“Home!”

Each word became a line of light. The vanished wall flickered back into being around the Blackwing’s neck, trapping it halfway through. The creature gave a cry that Rowan felt in his teeth.

It was not the cry of an animal.

It sounded like a thousand people trying to remember the same name.

The star-map hovered before Rowan. Its brightest road ran across the floor, climbed the Blackwing’s beak, and vanished between its eyes. Beyond the creature, the name SILAS burned like a promise.

Rook saw the path.

“No.”

“He is alive.”

“The map may be showing you bait.”

“It may be showing me Silas.”

“That is not a meaningful distinction to a trap.”

The Blackwing tore one wing free. A wave of Gloam swept through the archive. Where it passed, students stumbled and grabbed at their heads.

One boy began shouting that he had forgotten his sister.

A Lamplighter forgot how to release her staff and clung to it in terror.

Pip ripped the copper coil from his belt. The memory device had become smaller since the night before, though no less insulting to the principles of engineering. He slammed it against the floor and turned a handle.

A clear tone rang out.

Rowan’s name flashed inside his mind.

Mira Quill.

Pippin Noll.

For an instant, each of them felt the others as fixed points, bright and undeniable.

Pip stared at the device in astonishment.

“It works.”

Mira kicked a Gloamer away from a fallen student. “Celebrate later.”

“I may not live long enough to become modest!”

The Blackwing opened its beak.

Darkness gathered inside.

Rowan understood the road.

It did not pass over the creature.

It passed in.

He caught Mira’s eye.

She knew what he was thinking and disliked it immediately.

“No.”

“My mother’s letter said—”

“Your mother’s letter was disturbingly accurate, not legally binding.”

“The road leads to Silas.”

“The road leads into a bird made of stolen people.”

Pip joined them, brass arm smoking. “To be fair, many roads in Greyhaven have worse reviews.”

Another section of wall vanished.

The tower groaned.

Rook shouted for everyone to evacuate to the eastern stair. Master Fen and the senior students held the namefire barrier, but the Blackwing was pulling more of itself through with every breath.

Rowan took Mira’s hand.

Not because he needed permission.

Because he was asking her to stand beside him.

She swore softly.

Then she tightened her grip.

Pip attached the memory anchor to all three of their coats with copper wire.

“If one of us forgets who we are,” he said, “the other two should shout.”

“That is your plan?” Mira asked.

“I also have a hammer.”

They ran.

The star-road caught beneath Rowan’s feet. The tilted archive disappeared. For three impossible steps he crossed the air, moving along a line only he could see. Mira followed. Pip followed less elegantly, dragged by the wire while apologizing to several laws of nature.

They reached the Blackwing’s beak.

It opened.

Rowan saw no throat inside.

He saw a street.

Snow fell between familiar rooftops. Vale & Son stood beneath a winter sky. Warm light shone from the windows.

Then the creature swallowed them.

They landed in the lost memory of home.

The street was silent. Snowflakes hung motionless in the air. Every doorway opened onto another stolen place: the bakery where the nameless man had worked, a soldier’s childhood bedroom, a bridge at sunset, a hospital corridor, Saint Orra’s Fold before it vanished.

The Blackwing was not carrying people.

It was carrying everything taken from them.

“Silas!” Rowan shouted.

His voice travelled through a hundred memories.

Somewhere a pen scratched across paper.

They found him inside the map shop.

Silas sat at the worktable, drawing a map of a place Rowan had never seen. The paper showed an enormous blue expanse beneath a white sun. Islands floated across it like green coins.

Silas looked up.

His face was intact.

His eyes were empty.

“Shop is closed,” he said.

Rowan’s relief broke against fear.

“It’s me.”

“I don’t know you.”

“My name is Rowan.”

“That sounds inconvenient.”

It was the sort of answer Silas might have given.

But there was no recognition behind it.

A Gloamer had taken Rowan from him.

Rowan stepped closer. “You hate cinnamon. You pretend it is because of the price, but it is because Grandfather made you eat six cinnamon buns after you lied about finishing a map.”

Silas’s pen stopped.

“You keep emergency money inside the atlas of northern ventilation shafts because nobody has opened it since 382.”

A flicker crossed his face.

“You snore when you sleep in chairs. You sing only when you think the printing press is louder than you. You told me maps are promises made to strangers.”

Silas gripped the table.

Rowan raised his brass staff.

Namefire required truth.

Not facts.

He searched for the thing beneath every argument, every silence, every locked door.

“You are the man who stayed.”

Blue flame ran along the staff.

The shop windows filled with light.

Silas gasped.

Memory returned to his eyes with such force that he nearly fell.

“Rowan.”

The name shattered the false street.

Snow rushed upward. Doors slammed. The stolen rooms began collapsing into Gloam.

Silas seized the unfinished map.

“We have to leave.”

“What is that place?”

“The world below.”

“There is a world below?”

“There is always a world below. That is how below works.”

Even terrified and half-forgotten, he remained Silas.

They ran through the unraveling memories. The star-road reappeared, but another figure stood across it.

Tall. Motionless. Wrapped in a coat made of black feathers.

Its face was hidden behind an ivory mask marked with seven empty circles.

The masked figure spoke with Elian Vale’s voice.

“Hello, Ro.”

Rowan stopped.

Silas shoved him behind his back.

“That is not her.”

The figure tilted its head.

“Not all of her.”

Mira raised her knife. “Who are you?”

“A door that learned to knock.”

The circles on the ivory mask filled one by one with blue light.

“The city calls us the Gloam because it is easier to fear a fog than remember a crime. Greyhaven was not built to protect its people from what lies below.”

The figure looked at Rowan.

“It was built to keep its people from returning.”

The Blackwing screamed around them.

The memory world split open.

Through the crack, Rowan saw the archive and the Lamplighters fighting to hold the tower together.

The masked figure stepped backward into darkness.

“Ask the headmistress why the sky is forbidden.”

Then it was gone.

Pip struck the memory anchor with his hammer.

The device released a burst of clear sound.

The four of them were thrown out of the Blackwing.

They crashed onto the archive floor as namefire exploded through the tower. Rook drove her staff into the creature’s beak and shouted a single word.

“MESSENGER!”

The Blackwing froze.

Its wings became paper.

Thousands of black feathers transformed into stolen letters, photographs, recipes, names, and maps. They rained across the city.

The creature vanished.

For a moment, Greyhaven was quiet.

Then the Night Bell rang a second time.

Far below, something answered.

The entire city tilted west.

Bridges snapped taut. Buildings groaned. People slid across streets as the broken seventh chain pulled free of the Gloam.

On Rowan’s star-map, the six remaining locks began to open.
