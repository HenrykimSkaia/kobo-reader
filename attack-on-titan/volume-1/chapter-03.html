Volume 1 · Chapter 3

The House Beneath the Ruins

A literary condensed recap

The Titan came down the street without haste.

That calm frightened Eren more than a charge would have. It did not move like an enemy rushing into battle. It moved like a creature for which the outcome had already been decided.

Eren pulled at the beam across his mother's body until the wood tore skin from his palms. Mikasa pulled beside him. Dust coated their clothes. Carla kept telling them to stop.

Eren would not.

He had spent so much of his childhood speaking about courage that the idea of retreat felt like betrayal. If something was wrong, you fought it. If someone was trapped, you saved them. The world was supposed to become bearable if you refused to surrender.

The beam did not care what he believed.

It would not move.

The Titan drew closer.

• • •

Hannes arrived like an answer.

To Eren he had always been one of the adults who made peace look permanent. Hannes laughed easily, drank with the other guards, and had lived long enough inside the walls to believe that danger belonged to stories and expeditions.

Now his blades were drawn.

Eren felt hope return so quickly it almost hurt.

A soldier was here.

An adult was here.

The impossible weight of the broken house no longer seemed like something two children had to solve.

Hannes looked at Carla, then at the approaching Titan.

He stepped forward.

For a few seconds Eren saw the shape of the story he wanted: Hannes would fight, the Titan would fall, Carla would be freed, and afterward everyone would remember how close disaster had come.

Then Hannes stopped.

The Titan was near enough now that imagination was no longer useful. Its size erased bravado. Its face carried no malice, only a terrible absence of human meaning.

Hannes understood what fighting it might cost.

Eren saw that understanding reach him.

Then Hannes made a different choice.

He seized Eren and Mikasa.

Eren screamed.

• • •

Hannes ran.

Eren twisted in his grip, reaching toward the ruins even as the street carried him away from them. He shouted for his mother. He shouted at Hannes. He shouted at the world itself, as if volume could change what strength had failed to change.

Carla became smaller behind them.

She had urged the children to go, but once they were beyond reach, something in her expression broke. The courage she had used for them no longer had an audience.

Eren saw only fragments after that: smoke, the Titan leaning down, Mikasa's rigid silence beside him.

He did not understand grief yet.

He understood helplessness.

That feeling entered him cleanly.

He had always believed weakness was something a person could overcome by wanting hard enough not to be weak. Now he discovered another truth. Sometimes the world was stronger than desire.

Hannes kept running.

Behind them, the house disappeared from view.

• • •

The evacuation routes were drowning in people.

Families pressed toward the inner gate carrying bags, blankets, injured relatives, and whatever pieces of ordinary life they had managed to seize before fleeing. Soldiers tried to control the flow while rumors passed faster than orders. More Titans had entered. The district could not be held. Everyone had to move.

Eren stopped fighting Hannes's grip.

His body became strangely quiet.

Mikasa stayed beside him, but neither child spoke. There were no words large enough for what had happened and no adult nearby who could explain it into something smaller.

Shiganshina burned behind them.

Eren stared toward the smoke until his eyes hurt.

Something inside him was changing.

Grief was too large to hold all at once. Fear was humiliating. But anger was simple. Anger gave shape to what had no shape. It gave direction to pain.

The Titans had crossed the wall.

They had taken his home.

They had shown him exactly how powerless he was.

Eren's hatred did not arrive as a speech or a sudden revelation. It hardened slowly while the refugees were pushed farther from everything they knew.

He would remember the moment not as the day he became brave, but as the day he decided that helplessness could not be allowed to remain permanent.

Somewhere beyond the smoke stood the creatures that had made him understand that.

One day, he promised himself, he would be strong enough to answer them.
