Volume 1 · Chapter 3

The House Beneath the Ruins

A condensed prose retelling

The footsteps came closer.

Eren kept pulling at the broken beam until his hands burned. Dust covered his face and arms. Beside him, Mikasa tried to lift the same weight with silent desperation.

It did not move.

Carla was pinned beneath the remains of the house, but she was still conscious. She told them again to run. Eren answered by gripping the timber harder.

Leaving her was impossible.

Then the footsteps stopped sounding distant.

A Titan appeared at the end of the street.

It moved with a terrible calm, stepping between ruined houses as though the destruction around it meant nothing. Its expression carried no anger and no urgency. That made it worse. There was no hatred in its face, only hunger and instinct.

Eren froze for a heartbeat.

Then someone landed beside them.

Hannes.

• • •

For years, Hannes had seemed almost too relaxed for a soldier. He joked with the neighborhood children, drank with the guards, and enjoyed the peace that the walls provided.

Now there was no wall between him and the thing approaching them.

He drew his blades.

Eren felt a rush of hope. An adult was here. A soldier was here. Someone who could fix what children could not.

Hannes moved toward the Titan.

Then he saw it clearly.

The distance between bravery and death became suddenly obvious.

His body stopped.

For one long second, Hannes stood between two choices: fight a creature he did not believe he could defeat, or save the children while there was still time.

He chose the children.

Eren screamed as Hannes seized him and Mikasa and began to run.

He fought against the soldier's grip, twisting desperately toward the wreckage.

His mother was still there.

She grew smaller behind them as Hannes carried the children through the street.

Carla watched them go.

Only after they had turned away did the strength leave her face.

• • •

Eren could do nothing.

That was the part he would remember most clearly.

Not the noise. Not the dust. Not even the size of the Titan.

He would remember his own helplessness.

From Hannes's arms, he watched the creature reach the ruins of his home. He shouted until his throat hurt, but distance swallowed his voice.

The world had always taught Eren that strength belonged to those willing to fight for freedom. In that moment, he understood how little strength he actually possessed.

The Titan lifted his mother from the wreckage.

Eren's mind rejected what followed before his eyes could.

Hannes kept running.

Mikasa said nothing.

Behind them, the last shape of Eren's childhood disappeared into smoke.

• • •

The evacuation routes were already overflowing.

Thousands of people pushed toward the inner gate, carrying children, bags, injured relatives, and whatever fragments of their lives they had managed to save. Soldiers tried to control the crowd while Titans spread farther into the district.

Eren no longer struggled against Hannes.

His body had gone quiet.

Something inside him had not.

Fear was still there. So was grief. But beneath both of them, another feeling had begun to form—hotter, simpler, and easier to hold.

Hatred.

He stared toward the smoke rising above Shiganshina and understood that the life he had known was finished.

The walls had failed.

His home was gone.

And somewhere beyond the chaos stood the creatures responsible.

Eren did not yet know what he would become strong enough to do.

He only knew that one day, helplessness would no longer be an answer.
