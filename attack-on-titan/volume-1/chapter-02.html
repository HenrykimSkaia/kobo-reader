Volume 1 · Chapter 2

The Day the Sky Opened

A condensed prose retelling

The first warning was not a bell.

It was a sound like the world itself being struck.

Eren turned toward Wall Maria just as a violent gust tore through the streets. Dust burst from rooftops. Birds scattered in every direction. People stopped where they stood, confused by the sudden shadow that stretched across the district.

Then Eren looked up.

A face towered above the wall.

For generations, the walls had been the tallest things in the human world. They were supposed to define the boundary between safety and death. Yet the creature beyond them stood higher still, its skinless features steaming in the sunlight as if it had risen from some impossible furnace.

No one moved at first.

The mind resisted what the eyes had already accepted.

A Titan had appeared where no Titan should have been able to stand.

• • •

The impact came moments later.

The outer gate of Shiganshina exploded inward beneath a single devastating strike. Stone and timber flew through the district. A cloud of dust swallowed entire streets, and the calm afternoon vanished beneath screams.

Eren felt the ground shake beneath his feet.

All his life he had hated the walls because they trapped him. Now, as he watched a breach open through the barrier, he understood why everyone else had trusted them.

Beyond that opening waited the world humanity had spent a century hiding from.

And it was coming inside.

People began to run.

Shopkeepers abandoned their stalls. Parents dragged children through the streets. Soldiers shouted orders that disappeared beneath the noise of collapsing buildings and terrified crowds. The district, so ordinary only minutes before, became a maze of smoke and panic.

Eren searched desperately for Mikasa.

She was already beside him.

Neither of them needed to say where they were going.

Home.

• • •

They ran against the flow of fleeing citizens.

Every step toward their neighborhood became harder. Broken roof tiles covered the streets. Smoke drifted between houses. Somewhere behind them, the first Titans were entering through the shattered gate.

Eren could hear people shouting that they had seen them.

He refused to look back.

His mother had been home.

That single thought pushed everything else away.

When they finally reached the street where the Yeager house stood, Eren slowed.

Something enormous had fallen across the neighborhood.

What had been a familiar row of homes was now broken timber, stone, and dust.

For an instant, Eren could not understand what he was seeing.

Then he saw his mother beneath the wreckage.

She was alive.

The relief lasted less than a second.

Her legs were trapped beneath the collapsed house.

Eren and Mikasa threw themselves at the beams, pulling with everything they had. Nothing moved.

Carla told them to leave.

Eren ignored her.

He pulled harder.

Somewhere nearby, a heavy footstep struck the ground.

Then another.

The rhythm was slow.

Unhurried.

Something was coming down the street.
