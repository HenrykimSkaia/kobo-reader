Volume 1 · Chapter 5

Five Years Behind the Wall

A literary condensed recap

Training began by making failure public.

Every weakness appeared in front of everyone: poor balance, weak legs, slow reactions, fear hidden badly behind jokes. The equipment they were expected to master was unforgiving. A cadet had to trust cables, gas, blades, and the position of his own body at the same time. Hesitation became instability. Instability became the ground.

Eren hit the ground often enough to hate it personally.

He had imagined military training as something pain could solve. Work harder. Endure more. Refuse to quit. Yet the machinery demanded precision as much as stubbornness, and there were days when his determination seemed to produce nothing except bruises.

That only made him more determined.

Mikasa adapted with an ease that irritated him almost as much as it impressed him. She moved with the calm economy of someone whose body understood instructions before others finished hearing them. Strength, balance, awareness—everything fit together.

Eren knew he should have been proud.

Sometimes he was.

Sometimes he wanted desperately not to need her.

Armin fought a different battle. His body rarely matched those around him, but he noticed things others missed. He remembered explanations, read situations early, and learned how thought could compensate for force.

The three of them were becoming soldiers in three different ways.

• • •

The years gave names to the strangers around them.

Jean was sharp-tongued and practical, openly interested in earning a place where the danger was lower and life more comfortable. His realism clashed naturally with Eren's fury. Their arguments became almost ritual, each one accusing the other of misunderstanding what survival required.

Sasha could turn food into a matter of life and death even when no one was actually starving. Connie moved quickly, spoke quickly, and often seemed to arrive at confidence before thought. Reiner carried himself with the steadiness of someone older than the rest, the kind of person others looked toward when training stopped being funny. Bertolt remained harder to read, present without demanding attention. Annie kept people at a deliberate distance and seemed unimpressed by almost everyone.

Training made them compete, but exhaustion made them familiar.

They learned one another's bad habits. They recognized footsteps in the dark. They knew who would complain during a long march and who would silently give someone else part of a ration.

Friendship arrived almost accidentally.

• • •

Eren's purpose did not soften with time.

When conversations turned toward the three military branches, he always came back to the Survey Corps. The idea baffled recruits who had spent years learning exactly how easily Titans could kill them.

The Military Police represented distance from the front, status, and the safety of the interior. The Garrison offered a more ordinary military life. The Survey Corps offered uncertainty and a high chance of dying beyond the walls.

To Eren, that was not the full equation.

The Survey Corps also represented movement.

Humanity could not remain forever inside shrinking circles of stone. If the walls were the only strategy, then the Titans had already won. Every year of safety would merely be another year spent waiting for the next breach.

He spoke with such conviction that even those who mocked him listened longer than they meant to.

Armin understood the older dream beneath Eren's anger. Somewhere beyond the walls were the places from their childhood book. Mikasa understood something more immediate: Eren would choose danger whether she approved or not.

• • •

Five years passed since the fall of Shiganshina.

By graduation, the recruits looked different in uniform, though not as different as they sometimes imagined. They had stronger bodies, quicker reflexes, and enough experience to know what they still did not know.

Rankings were announced. Futures began dividing.

Those near the top could pursue the Military Police. Others considered the Garrison. Conversations that had once been abstract became decisions with consequences.

Eren's choice was already made.

He would go outward.

The night before those futures truly began, the cadets ate together and argued in the familiar ways. Jean challenged him. Eren refused to bend. Around them, others listened, laughed, or quietly reconsidered what courage meant.

For one evening, the war felt far away.

The wall stood.

The lamps burned.

They were young enough to believe that finishing training had moved them from danger into readiness.

Eren looked toward the dark beyond Trost and remembered another wall, another ordinary day, another moment when everyone had believed disaster belonged somewhere else.

Five years was a long time.

It was not long enough to make him forget.
