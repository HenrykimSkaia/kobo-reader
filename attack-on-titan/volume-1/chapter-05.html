Volume 1 · Chapter 5

Five Years Behind the Wall

A condensed prose retelling

Training reduced every recruit to the same simple truths: balance, endurance, fear, pain, repetition.

The instructors cared little about dreams. They wanted bodies that could obey under pressure and minds that would not collapse at the sight of death.

Eren struggled, failed, learned, and tried again.

The equipment demanded absolute control. A moment of hesitation could send a cadet crashing to the ground. A mistake in real combat would be worse.

Mikasa adapted with frightening ease. Strength, balance, judgment—everything seemed to come naturally to her. Others noticed. Eren did too, though admiration mixed with frustration whenever she treated him as someone who still needed protecting.

Armin survived by thinking. Where others relied on power, he watched patterns, remembered instructions, and learned how to compensate for what his body lacked.

• • •

Years passed.

The recruits who had once been strangers became rivals, companions, and, in some cases, friends. Jean spoke openly about choosing the Military Police and earning a safer life in the interior. Connie covered uncertainty with humor. Sasha's appetite made her impossible to ignore. Reiner carried himself like an older brother, dependable when others lost focus. Bertolt remained quieter, often standing just outside the center of attention. Annie kept most people at a distance.

They argued about why anyone would willingly face Titans.

For Eren, the answer never changed.

He remembered Shiganshina.

He remembered being carried away while everything behind him burned.

The others sometimes heard obsession in his voice. Eren heard clarity.

Humanity could not spend eternity hiding.

• • •

By graduation, the children who entered training had become soldiers.

The rankings were announced. Some celebrated because high placement opened the path to the Military Police. Others weighed the Garrison against the Survey Corps and tried to decide how much danger they could accept.

Eren had already decided years ago.

He would join the Survey Corps.

His certainty affected the others more than he realized. Jean mocked it, but listened. Armin understood it. Mikasa disliked the danger but would not abandon him.

For one brief evening, the graduates were allowed to imagine that the hardest part might be over.

They ate, argued, and spoke about their futures.

Five years had passed since the fall of Shiganshina.

The walls had stood untouched since then.

It was long enough for some people to begin believing that disaster had become history.

Eren knew better.
