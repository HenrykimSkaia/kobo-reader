Volume 1 · Chapter 4

The Price of Survival

A literary condensed recap

The river carried the refugees away from Shiganshina, but not away from what had happened there.

Eren sat among bodies pressed shoulder to shoulder inside the evacuation vessel. Nobody had enough room to be alone, yet grief made islands of them all. Some people cried openly. Others stared ahead with the stillness of those who had exhausted even panic. Children kept asking questions that adults could not answer.

Mikasa remained beside Eren. Armin was near them, pale and emptied of speech.

Behind the vessel, smoke rose over the district where their homes had been.

Eren watched until distance erased the streets, then the rooftops, then almost everything except the wall itself.

He had once hated Wall Maria because it kept humanity from the world.

Now he hated it for failing.

• • •

The surviving territory was not prepared for so many displaced people.

Refugees arrived carrying what they could lift and memories they could not put down. Warehouses emptied. Shelters filled. Food became arithmetic: how many people, how much grain, how many days.

Eren learned that disaster did not end when the screaming stopped.

It changed clothes.

Hunger replaced panic. Lines replaced escape routes. The survivors of Wall Maria discovered that being alive could still mean being unwanted. People who had lost nothing spoke bitterly about rations. Those who had lost everything learned to lower their eyes and wait.

Eren despised waiting.

He despised the bowls of thin food, the crowded rooms, the whispers that treated refugees like a burden. Most of all, he despised the helpless logic beneath it all: there was not enough land, not enough food, not enough strength to take back what had been lost.

The world seemed determined to keep proving the same lesson to him.

Humanity was weak.

• • •

The following year, adults among the displaced population were sent into a massive attempt to reclaim territory.

The operation was spoken about as necessity, but the children understood the silence around it. Too many people were being fed inside too little land. Reclamation and sacrifice became difficult to separate.

Many who left never returned.

For Armin, the loss became personal. The small family that had protected him after the fall of Shiganshina was reduced again. He did not rage the way Eren did. His grief folded inward, sharpening the thoughtful quiet that had always set him apart.

Mikasa responded differently. Her world had already narrowed once before, and she seemed determined not to let it narrow again. Staying near Eren became more than affection. It was a way of refusing another disappearance.

Eren understood neither of them completely.

His own grief was easier when it became anger.

Anger could be pointed somewhere.

• • •

When the military opened its training corps to a new class of cadets, Eren did not hesitate.

Years earlier he had wanted to join the Survey Corps because the world beyond the walls called to him. He had imagined exploration as an act of freedom.

Now freedom meant something harsher.

It meant refusing to accept that humanity's proper place was retreat.

It meant becoming strong enough that the next Titan would not find him pulling uselessly at a beam while someone he loved waited to die.

Mikasa enrolled too.

She did not need to explain why.

Armin followed, though everyone—including Armin himself—knew that physical strength would never be his greatest weapon. He carried fear with him into training, but he also carried the memory of the book he and Eren had once read together.

The ocean still existed somewhere.

That mattered.

• • •

On the first day, the recruits stood in formation beneath an instructor whose voice seemed designed to strip away every illusion they had brought with them.

Names were demanded. Weakness was exposed. Pride was treated like something that had to be broken before it could become useful.

Eren looked along the line of unfamiliar faces.

Some were there for security. Some for status. Some because military service offered food and purpose. A few were there because they believed in fighting Titans.

They were children pretending not to be children.

Soon they would be taught to move through the air with steel at their sides, to aim for the narrow place where a Titan could be killed, to make decisions while terrified.

Eren felt fear too.

But for the first time since Shiganshina fell, fear was no longer the only thing waiting ahead of him.

There was training.

There was effort.

There was the possibility of becoming someone his younger self would have needed on that ruined street.

For Eren, that possibility was enough to begin.
