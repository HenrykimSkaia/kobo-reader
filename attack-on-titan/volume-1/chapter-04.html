Volume 1 · Chapter 4

The Price of Survival

A condensed prose retelling

The boats carried the survivors away from Shiganshina, but distance did nothing to make the destruction feel less real.

Eren sat among strangers pressed shoulder to shoulder, staring back toward the smoke. Mikasa remained beside him. Armin was there too, pale and silent. Around them, grief took a hundred different forms: children calling for parents who would never answer, adults clutching whatever they had managed to save, soldiers trying to sound certain while knowing nothing was certain anymore.

Behind them, Wall Maria had failed.

A world that had seemed permanent had ended in a single day.

• • •

Inside the remaining territory, the refugees discovered another kind of danger.

There were too many mouths and too little food. Warehouses emptied. Shelters overflowed. The people who had once lived comfortably behind the inner walls now looked at the displaced population with suspicion, resentment, and fear.

Eren heard adults argue over rations. He saw exhausted families reduced to waiting in lines for scraps. No Titan stood over them, yet humiliation followed the refugees everywhere.

He began to understand that survival alone was not freedom.

The following year, many of the displaced were sent on a desperate operation to reclaim lost land. Few returned. The official language called it necessity. To Eren, it looked like another defeat hidden behind careful words.

Armin lost the last of his immediate family in that effort.

The three children carried their losses differently. Armin became quieter. Mikasa concentrated on staying close to Eren, as if keeping him alive could somehow keep the remains of her world intact.

Eren did neither.

He sharpened his grief into a promise.

• • •

When the military began accepting new cadets, Eren was ready.

He had once dreamed of joining the Survey Corps because he wanted to see what existed beyond the walls. That dream remained, but it had changed. Freedom was no longer only curiosity. It had become defiance.

Mikasa chose the same path, though her reason was simpler: wherever Eren went, danger followed, and she intended to follow too.

Armin joined despite knowing he was not physically strong. Fear had never left him. Neither had the memory of the book that once showed him oceans and distant lands.

On the day they entered training, Eren stood among dozens of recruits and looked at the uniforms, the instructors, and the hard faces around him.

They were children trying to become soldiers before the world found another way to destroy them.

For the first time since Shiganshina fell, Eren felt that his anger had somewhere to go.
