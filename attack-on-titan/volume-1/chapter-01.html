The Boy Inside the Cage

A condensed prose retelling

Eren awoke beneath the tree with tears on his face.

For a few moments, he remained still, staring through the branches at a sky too blue to explain the sorrow lingering inside him. He could not remember the dream. Only the feeling remained—the strange certainty that he had lost something before he even knew what it was.

Mikasa stood nearby, watching him with quiet concern. Together, they returned toward Shiganshina, the district that extended from Wall Maria like a small stone pocket attached to an enormous fortress.

To most people, the walls represented peace. They had protected humanity for generations from the Titans wandering outside. Their size was so overwhelming that few citizens questioned whether they could ever fall.

Eren saw them differently.

Whenever he looked upward, he did not see protection. He saw the edge of a cage.

• • •

That afternoon, the Survey Corps returned from an expedition beyond the walls. The soldiers entered the district exhausted and wounded. Some rode with empty saddles beside them. Families pushed through the crowd, searching desperately for familiar faces.

The sight should have convinced Eren that the outside world meant only death. Instead, it deepened his anger. The soldiers had failed, but at least they had stepped beyond the walls. They had seen a world that most citizens were too frightened to imagine.

Armin understood that longing. He possessed a forbidden book describing distant oceans, burning lands, frozen plains, and other wonders beyond humanity’s prison. To the two boys, those places were more than geography. They were proof that life was supposed to be larger than the narrow streets of Shiganshina.

Yet dreaming about freedom made them targets. Other children mocked Armin and attacked him for questioning the safety of the walls. Eren defended him with more courage than skill, while Mikasa ended the confrontation through sheer presence.

• • •

Later, Eren returned home to find his mother, Carla, waiting. When she learned of his desire to join the Survey Corps, fear overcame her. She knew what happened to those who ventured outside.

Eren’s father, Grisha, did not forbid the dream. Before leaving on business, he promised that one day he would show Eren what was hidden in the basement beneath their home.

The promise remained in Eren’s thoughts long after his father departed.

That evening, life inside Shiganshina continued as it always had. Merchants called to customers. Soldiers drank at their posts. Children ran between houses while sunlight warmed the tiled roofs.

The walls stood above them, silent and absolute.

No one yet understood how quickly something that had lasted a hundred years could disappear.
