Volume 1 · Chapter 7

The First Battle

A literary condensed recap

The first battle taught them how little training resembled survival.

In exercises, failure had an ending. An instructor shouted. A cadet hit the ground. Someone laughed later over dinner. On the streets of Trost, failure did not stop when the lesson became clear.

Eren's squad crossed the rooftops with practiced speed, using chimneys and stone walls as anchors. For the first few moments, movement itself offered reassurance. Their bodies knew what to do. Cables fired. Gas pushed them forward. Formation gave shape to fear.

Then a Titan appeared where it should not have been.

The formation broke.

Another threat cut off the route ahead. Someone changed direction too late. A cry ended abruptly.

The battlefield became fragments.

Eren saw people he knew vanish from the fight faster than his mind could register their deaths. There was no time to stop and mourn. Every second spent looking backward created another danger in front of him.

He had once imagined rage would make combat simple.

It did not.

Hatred could not create gas where there was none. It could not repair a bad angle or return a fallen soldier to the roof.

The Titans did not care how strongly he wanted humanity to win.

• • •

Armin's fear became physical.

His thoughts still moved, but his body stopped obeying them. The streets below seemed too narrow, the Titans too large, the possibilities too many. Every calculation ended in another way to die.

He had always known he was afraid of Titans.

Knowing was different from standing close enough to hear them breathe.

One moment he was trying to move with the others.

The next he was trapped.

A Titan lifted him toward its mouth.

Panic erased strategy. Armin saw teeth, darkness, sky. He understood with terrible clarity that all the places from the old book—the ocean, the distant landscapes, the world beyond the walls—might continue to exist without him ever seeing them.

Then Eren came back.

There was no careful plan in the rescue. It was the same part of Eren that had once thrown himself into fights for Armin as a child, now armed with military equipment and surrounded by consequences.

He reached his friend.

He forced Armin away from the Titan.

For one instant, it worked.

Then the cost arrived.

• • •

Armin hit safety hard enough to lose the air from his lungs.

When he turned back, Eren was no longer where a living person should have been.

The scene made no sense at first.

Eren was the one who always moved forward. Eren was the one who spoke as if death could be defeated by refusing to respect it. Eren was the one who had spent five years building himself around the promise that he would fight.

Now he was caught in the thing he hated.

Armin reached for him from too far away.

Between them lay only a few meters and an impossible distance.

For a heartbeat their childhood seemed to return: two boys bent over forbidden pages, speaking about a world neither had seen. Back then the walls had seemed like the great obstacle. If they could somehow get past them, life would begin.

The dream had survived hunger, training, grief, and five years of waiting.

Now it balanced on a moment neither of them had imagined.

Eren used what strength remained to make sure Armin stayed out of reach.

Then the Titan's jaws closed.

• • •

Noise returned all at once.

Gas hissed. Soldiers shouted. Titans moved through the streets. Somewhere a roof cracked beneath an impact.

Armin remained still.

There was blood on him that was not his.

His mind tried to reject what his eyes had seen, searching for another explanation, another sequence of events in which Eren would appear on a nearby roof and shout at him for freezing.

Nothing changed.

The battle continued without waiting for grief.

That was its cruelty.

Shiganshina had taught Eren that the world could destroy an ordinary day without warning.

Trost taught Armin something equally brutal: the world could take a person in the middle of a promise and keep moving.

Above the district, smoke climbed into the sky.

Across the rooftops, the surviving cadets fought to remain alive.

Somewhere else in the city, Mikasa was still fighting, unaware that the person she had built so much of her life around had disappeared into the chaos.

And Armin, who had always believed Eren possessed the courage he lacked, was left with the terrible question of what courage meant when the brave person was gone.

The Battle of Trost had barely begun.

For the survivors, childhood was already over.
