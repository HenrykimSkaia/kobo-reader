Volume 1 · Chapter 6

The Enemy Returns

A literary condensed recap

The morning at Trost was ordinary enough to be insulting.

The newly graduated cadets worked along the wall beneath a clear sky, checking equipment and talking about the futures they had spent years preparing to choose. The great stones beneath their boots seemed solid. The district below moved through another routine day.

Eren had learned to distrust routine.

Still, even he did not expect the world to split open twice in the same way.

Heat struck first.

Then steam.

A violent burst of air rolled over the wall, forcing soldiers to turn away. When Eren looked back, five years vanished from his life.

The Colossal Titan stood before him.

For an instant he was a child again, staring upward while Shiganshina collapsed.

Memory came in flashes: falling stone, smoke, his mother's face, Hannes running.

Then training interrupted memory.

His hands moved.

Blades came free.

This time he was armed.

• • •

Eren launched himself toward the giant.

Gas hissed through his maneuvering gear. Steel cables struck the wall and pulled him into open air. Years of drills condensed into instinct: angle, speed, distance, momentum.

Below him, soldiers shouted. Above him, steam poured from the Titan's immense body.

Eren did not think of fear as something that had disappeared. Fear was there, sharp and physical. But now it had company.

He had wanted this chance for years.

Not this battlefield, not these people in danger—but the chance to stand in front of the thing that had once reduced him to helplessness and discover whether he was still that child.

He closed the distance.

The Titan seemed almost unreal at close range, less like flesh than some enormous mechanism built from heat and muscle.

Eren searched for an opening.

Then the creature vanished into steam.

One moment it filled the sky.

The next there was only heat and white vapor.

Eren passed through empty space.

There was no body to strike and no time to understand.

The purpose of the attack had already been achieved.

• • •

The breach below turned Trost into a battlefield.

Alarms spread. Civilians flooded evacuation routes. Units were reassigned before anyone had time to absorb the first orders. The cadets who had spent the previous evening debating careers were suddenly being directed toward Titans.

The transformation was brutal.

There was no ceremony marking the end of training.

No moment in which someone declared them ready.

The enemy had arrived, and readiness was now whatever they could produce.

Jean's practical confidence hardened into concentration. Connie stopped joking. Armin's eyes moved constantly, taking in routes, buildings, distances, threats.

Mikasa was sent to another assignment.

Eren disliked it immediately.

He had grown up resenting the way she protected him, yet the battlefield made separation feel wrong. Five years of shared training had created habits deeper than argument. He expected her nearby even when he complained that she was nearby too often.

Now orders pulled them apart.

Mikasa left reluctantly.

Eren told himself that was what soldiers did.

• • •

From the rooftops, Trost looked both familiar and transformed.

Smoke moved between buildings. People fled along narrow streets. Titans wandered through spaces built for human scale, making houses appear fragile and temporary.

Eren's squad prepared to enter its assigned sector.

He looked at the others.

They were people he had trained beside, eaten beside, argued beside. Until that morning their deaths had been theoretical.

The realization unsettled him more than he expected.

For years Eren had imagined combat as a clean confrontation between humanity and the creatures that had destroyed his home.

From the wall, the conflict had looked simple.

Down in the streets, it would not be.

The Titans were not waiting politely for brave people to attack them one at a time.

There would be confusion. Bad angles. Empty gas. Fear. Mistakes.

People would die before anyone understood what had gone wrong.

Eren knew all of this in theory.

Then the order came to move.

His squad launched into the city.

The theory ended.
