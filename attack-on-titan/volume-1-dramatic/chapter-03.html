Volume 1 · Dramatic Edition · Chapter 3

The House Beneath the Ruins

A dramatic literary recap

Eren pulled until the skin split across his palms.

The beam remained where it was.

Dust clung to his mother's hair. Blood darkened the stones beneath her. Carla tried to keep her voice steady, but fear had changed it into something thin and urgent. She told Eren and Mikasa to leave. She told them again when they pretended not to hear.

The Titan came closer.

It walked without hurry, its bare feet crushing tiles and broken furniture beneath them. Its expression was almost pleasant. That was the horror of it. There was no rage in its face, no cruelty Eren could fight or understand. It approached the ruined house with the mindless certainty of hunger.

Steel flashed beside them.

Hannes landed in the street with his blades drawn.

Hope struck Eren so sharply it hurt. Hannes was a soldier. An adult. A man who had once boasted that the Garrison had grown lazy only because the walls were too strong to need heroes. Now he stood between the children and the Titan, and for a heartbeat Eren believed the world had corrected itself.

Hannes charged.

Then the Titan looked at him.

The soldier's courage broke before his body did. He saw the distance between his blades and that enormous mouth. He saw Carla trapped behind him and two children waiting to learn whether bravery could defeat terror.

Hannes made his choice.

He seized Eren and Mikasa and ran.

Eren screamed until his voice tore. He struck Hannes's arms, kicked, twisted, begged. The ruined house receded behind them. His mother became smaller in the smoke.

Carla watched her children being carried away.

Only when she believed they could no longer hear her did her face collapse.

The Titan reached down.

Eren saw its hand close around her body. He saw his mother lifted from the remains of their home as easily as a doll. The world narrowed to her face, the smoke, and the red cavern of the creature's mouth.

Hannes kept running.

Mikasa had gone utterly still.

Eren did not close his eyes.

Something inside him was crushed before the Titan's jaws came together. Childhood, perhaps. Or the belief that wanting something badly enough could give a person the strength to save it.

At the evacuation canal, soldiers forced survivors onto overcrowded boats. The district burned behind them. Through the shattered gate, more Titans wandered into Shiganshina beneath the vast, indifferent sky.

Eren stood at the stern until the smoke swallowed everything.

He had wanted to leave the walls because he dreamed of freedom.

Now the walls had opened, and the outside world had taken his home.

Grief filled him first. Then shame. Then a heat so fierce it gave shape to both.

He made no grand speech. No one around him would have heard it.

But in the silence behind his teeth, Eren gave hatred a purpose.

One day, he would become strong enough that helplessness would never again be the final answer.
