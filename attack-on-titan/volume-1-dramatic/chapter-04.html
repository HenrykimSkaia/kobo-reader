The World After Shiganshina

A dramatic literary recap

The survivors learned that escaping death was not the same as being saved.

Boats carried them north through the inner gate, away from the smoke of Shiganshina and into territory still protected by Wall Rose. They arrived with ash on their clothes and almost nothing in their hands. The inner districts received them with hurried shelters, ration lines, and the strained politeness of people wondering how much food the refugees would consume.

There were too many mouths.

Warehouses emptied. Bread hardened. Soup became colored water. Families slept shoulder to shoulder on floors while officials spoke of order and necessity.

Eren moved through those months as though a blade had been left inside him. Sometimes anger gave him strength. Sometimes it left him exhausted. At night he dreamed of his mother's hand reaching from beneath the beams, and every morning he woke furious that the world had continued.

Armin had lost his home as well. His grandfather was the last family he had left. Mikasa remained near Eren, never crowding him, never far enough that he could disappear.

Then the government announced a campaign to reclaim Wall Maria.

The refugees were told it was an opportunity to recover their land. Everyone understood the quieter truth: there was not enough food to keep them all alive.

Adults who had never held weapons were sent beyond the gates. Armin's grandfather went with them.

Most did not return.

The operation was called a failure. The ration lines became shorter.

Armin wept without sound. Eren stood beside him and discovered that rage could become cold.

Two years later, the three of them entered military training.

The instructor who received the new cadets had a voice designed to strip away pride. He walked among them, demanding names, hometowns, reasons. Some recruits trembled. Others shouted too loudly. Each had arrived carrying a private bargain with fear.

Jean Kirstein wanted the safety and status of the Military Police. Connie Springer hid nervousness behind humor. Sasha Blouse nearly began her military career by refusing to surrender food. Reiner Braun stood broad and dependable; Bertolt Hoover lingered quietly beside him. Annie Leonhart watched everyone with eyes that seemed already tired of the world.

Eren declared that he would join the Survey Corps and fight Titans.

The instructor did not praise him.

Training did not care about declarations.

It cared whether a cadet could remain upright in maneuvering gear, whether hands remembered their work under pressure, whether lungs kept drawing breath after the body demanded surrender.

On Eren's first balance test, he failed.

He hung upside down while the others watched.

For one sickening moment he wondered whether hatred had brought him here only to reveal that he was useless.

Then the equipment was inspected.

A damaged belt had betrayed him.

With proper gear, Eren lifted himself and held steady.

The achievement was small. It felt enormous.

For the first time since Shiganshina, the future did not look like something merely approaching him.

It looked like something he might be able to meet.
