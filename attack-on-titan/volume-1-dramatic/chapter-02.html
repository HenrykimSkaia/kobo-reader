Volume 1 · Dramatic Edition · Chapter 2

The Giant Above the Wall

A dramatic literary recap

The explosion did not sound like thunder.

Thunder rolled and faded. This noise struck the district like a hammer against the bones of the earth.

Windows rattled. Roof tiles leapt. A wall of hot wind tore through the street and sent birds spinning from the eaves. Eren turned toward the southern gate, one hand raised against the dust.

Then a shadow climbed over the city.

A head appeared above Wall Maria.

For one impossible moment, nobody moved. The wall was the tallest object in the human world. Children grew up believing even the sky had to bend around it. Yet the creature beyond it looked down upon the district as if the ancient barrier were no more than a garden fence.

Its face had no skin. Red muscle tightened around a terrible grin. Steam poured from its body in white rivers, and its eyes moved over Shiganshina without surprise, hatred, or mercy.

The Colossal Titan lifted its foot.

Eren saw the motion but could not understand it quickly enough.

The gate vanished in a burst of stone.

The shock wave threw people to the ground. Houses split open. Fragments of masonry screamed across the district and struck roofs, streets, bodies. For a heartbeat the entire city disappeared inside dust.

Then came the silence—the stunned, breathless silence of thousands of people realizing the same thing.

The wall had been breached.

Screaming began everywhere at once.

Merchants abandoned their carts. Mothers dragged children through alleys. Soldiers shouted contradictory orders while the first alarm bells rang from the towers. Eren heard someone cry that Titans were entering through the gate. Another voice insisted it was impossible, even as the ground trembled beneath approaching footsteps.

Mikasa seized Eren's arm.

Their eyes met.

Home.

They ran against the fleeing crowd.

Every instinct demanded that they turn back, but Eren could think only of his mother. He forced his way through smoke and falling debris while Mikasa stayed beside him with the grim, wordless focus that had always made adults underestimate her only once.

The streets grew less familiar the closer they came to the house. A roof had collapsed across one lane. A wagon burned in another. Somewhere behind them, a Titan laughed with the empty delight of a child.

Eren rounded the final corner and stopped so violently that Mikasa nearly struck him.

Their house was gone.

A massive stone had smashed through the roof, crushing the rooms where they had eaten breakfast, argued, and waited for Grisha to return. Beneath the broken beams lay Carla Yeager.

Alive.

Trapped.

Eren fell beside her and pulled at the timber. Mikasa joined him. The beam did not shift.

Carla told them to run.

Eren pulled harder.

The ground shook.

At the far end of the street, something enormous stepped through the smoke and turned its smiling face toward them.
