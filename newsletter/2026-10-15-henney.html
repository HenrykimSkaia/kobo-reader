영화 속 드로리안이 하늘에 오르다: 전기 플라잉카 레이싱 출전

2026-10-15 · One topic, three parts · English reading

Source: Interesting Engineering

Deep Reading

Part 1 — 뉴스 본문: 영화 속 드로리안, 진짜 하늘에 오르다

If you have ever watched Back to the Future, you know the scene. Marty McFly climbs into a silver car with doors that open upward like wings, and the car rises into the sky. For more than forty years, people have pictured a DeLorean flying through the air. This month, that picture came one step closer to reality.

On October 13, the DeLorean Motor Company announced at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 in San Francisco that it will join the Airspeeder International Series, the world's first racing championship for electric flying cars. DeLorean will be the first American team, or "constructor," in the series. The DeLorean Airspeeder Team is scheduled to make its debut when the 2026-27 season opens in November 2026.

Airspeeder is not ordinary racing. The race vehicles are electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft, called eVTOLs for short. They lift off like helicopters, fly like airplanes, and run on batteries instead of fuel. "People have pictured a DeLorean in the sky for forty years," said Cameron Wynne, DeLorean's chief brand officer. "We decided it was time to stop picturing it."

For its first season, DeLorean will not race its own design. Every team will use the same standardized racing aircraft, the Airspeeder Mk3C. When all machines are equal, the contest comes down to strategy, skill, and teamwork. Then, for the 2027-28 season, DeLorean plans to design its own custom racer together with engineering students from UC Berkeley and Georgia Tech.

One detail is worth keeping in mind: DeLorean has not announced any flying car for ordinary customers. This is a racing program, not a product launch. What it promises is something different, a role in developing electric aviation through competition.

Part 2 — 더 깊이 알기: 왜 레이싱이 비행기의 미래를 바꾸는가

Why would anyone build a race for flying cars? The answer comes from the history of car racing. Formula 1 cars are not normal cars, but the technology tested on the track, from disc brakes to hybrid engines, eventually finds its way into the cars your family drives. Airspeeder is trying to do the same thing for the sky: use the pressure of competition to make electric flight better, faster.

Electric flight is hard because of one stubborn problem: batteries. A jet engine stores energy in fuel, and fuel is light for the energy it carries. Batteries are heavy. In a small electric aircraft, most of the weight can be batteries, which leaves little room for passengers or cargo. That is why the first electric aircraft are small racers and delivery drones rather than jumbo jets. Racing engineers must squeeze the most flight time out of every kilogram.

The eVTOL racers solve a second puzzle with their design. A helicopter uses one big rotor to go straight up. An airplane uses a wing to glide forward efficiently. The Airspeeder Mk3C uses many small electric propellers spread across its body. Eight motors mean eight chances to keep flying if one fails, and a computer can adjust each motor's speed hundreds of times per second to keep the craft balanced. Imagine balancing eight spinning tops with a laptop brain. That is roughly what the flight computer does.

The race itself adds a third dimension. Instead of circling an asphalt track, the racers fly through courses marked by virtual gates, invisible checkpoints in the air defined by electronic positioning. Onboard sensors and collision-avoidance systems watch the space around the aircraft, because in a three-dimensional race, a rival can be above you, below you, or beside you. The pilots must think like aviators and racers at the same time.

Part 3 — 그 다음은: 레이싱에서 일상 하늘로

What comes next is the part that should interest you most. For the 2027-28 season, DeLorean will work with engineering students from two famous American universities, UC Berkeley and Georgia Tech, to design its own racing aircraft. The students who will solve the battery, balance, and safety puzzles are people only a few years older than you. The flight path of the future may literally be drawn by today's university students.

Cameron Wynne also said racing is a chance to help shape the rules for flying cars, not just the machines. Before air taxis can fly over a city, someone must answer hard questions: How quiet must they be? How low may they fly over houses? Who is responsible if a computer is doing the flying? A race series, with its closed courses and strict safety checks, is a good laboratory for those answers.

History suggests this works. In the 1930s, air races like the Schneider Trophy pushed engineers to build faster seaplanes, and those designs later shaped the airplanes everyone flies in today. The technology you race today becomes the transportation of tomorrow. If electric flying cars ever become as normal as bicycles, people will trace the story back to series like Airspeeder.

Still, keep one foot on the ground. Many details remain unknown: who the pilots will be, what DeLorean's own racer will look like, and when air taxis will truly be common. And remember, no one has announced a flying car you can buy. What this story really teaches is something better than a product: breakthroughs in aviation often begin as wild ideas that serious engineers decide to test. That is a path you can walk yourself, one physics class at a time.

Essay 과제 — 에세이를 써보세요.

Imagine you are a 20-year-old engineering student at Georgia Tech. The DeLorean team has just invited you to join the project that will design the team's own racing aircraft for the 2027-28 Airspeeder season. Your first task is to pick one problem to work on: the batteries, the propellers, the safety systems, or the race strategy. Which one would you choose, and how would your choice help the team win?

Write 150–200 words in English. Include: which problem you would pick and why it matters most; what you would test first; and if the test result surprised you, what that would tell the engineers.
