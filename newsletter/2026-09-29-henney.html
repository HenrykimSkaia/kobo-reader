세계 최초의 플라잉카, 생산 시작

2026-09-29 · One topic, three parts · English reading

Deep Reading

Part 1 — 뉴스 본문: 무슨 일이 일어났나

For a long time, flying cars were only in movies. Now they are becoming real.

Alef Aeronautics is a startup company in California. On December 9, 2025, it started to build the world's first flying car. The car's name is Model A. The first cars are built by hand at the company's factory in San Mateo, California. Building by hand is slow, but it lets the engineers check every single part with great care.

The story started in 2015. Four founders — Jim Dukhovny, Constantine Kisly, Pavel Markin, and Oleg Petrov — loved the 1985 movie Back to the Future. In the movie, cars fly freely in the sky. The founders asked a simple question: why can't we build that for real? They drew their first design on a napkin in a cafe. A napkin sketch became a company.

From the start, they had three clear goals. First, the car must drive on normal roads, just like a regular car. Second, it must be fully electric — no gas, no noisy engine. Third, it must take off straight up, so it never needs a runway. Many flying car ideas failed because they missed one of these three. Alef wanted all three.

Within a year, the team built a small test version. It was good enough to impress investors, and money came in to continue the work. Since then, the team has worked for ten years — through thousands of tests, failures, and redesigns — to make the real car.

The Model A can drive on roads like a normal car. It can also take off straight up into the sky, like a helicopter. It does not need a runway. On the road, it can drive about 200 miles (321 km). In the sky, it can fly about 110 miles (177 km). Its top speed in the air is 110 mph (177 km/h) — faster than any car on a highway.

This dream is very old. In 1894 — more than 130 years ago — an inventor named Hiram Maxim drew a flying car with four wheels. His idea never flew. For over a century, the flying car stayed in drawings and movies. Now, Alef is making it real.

About 3,500 people have already ordered a Model A, even though each one costs about $300,000. But there is still one big step left: the car needs full approval from the FAA, the group that controls flight safety in the United States. Until then, you cannot buy one and fly to school. Not yet.

Part 2 — 더 깊이 알기: 어떻게 나는 걸까

How does a flying car actually fly? Let's look closer.

The Model A does not have one big engine like an airplane. It has many small electric motors. Each motor turns a propeller. Together, all the propellers lift the car straight up into the sky. If one motor has a problem, the others can keep working. That is much safer than depending on a single engine.

The most important trick is the takeoff. The Model A rises straight up, like a helicopter. It does not need a runway at all. This matters a lot in cities, where there is no empty land for runways. A flying car could take off from a parking lot, a rooftop, or a wide street.

Here is the clever part. After takeoff, the whole car tilts. It turns 90 degrees, so it lies on its side. Now the sides of the car work like wings, pushing against the air to move forward. The cockpit — where the driver sits — stays upright. So the driver always sits straight and comfortable, even while the car flies on its side. It looks strange, but it works.

Why electric? Electric motors are quiet and clean. They do not need gas, and they are much simpler than gas engines, with fewer parts that can break. But there is a big problem: batteries are heavy, and heavy things are hard to fly. A flying machine must be as light as possible.

That is why the Model A uses carbon fiber. Carbon fiber is very strong, but very light — much lighter than steel. Race cars and airplanes use it for the same reason. Every kilogram saved means the car can fly a little farther.

Building a flying car is not just about technology. Safety rules are just as important. The FAA must check every part of the car before ordinary people can use it. Engineers must prove the car is safe in strong wind, heavy rain, and even when parts fail. This takes a long time, because a problem in the sky is much more dangerous than a problem on the road.

Then there is the price. The Model A costs about $300,000 today — more than ten normal cars. But engineers think the price will fall when factories can build many cars at once, just like what happened with computers and mobile phones. The first computers filled whole rooms and cost a fortune. Now everyone carries one in a pocket.

Part 3 — 그 다음은: 하늘을 향한 남은 과제들

So the flying car can take off, tilt, and fly. What happens now?

The biggest gate is approval. The FAA must test the Model A in many difficult situations — strong wind, heavy rain, engine trouble — before ordinary people can fly it. This is slow, careful work, and nobody can skip it. Alef has already done test flights, but flying over a busy city is very different from flying over an empty field. One accident could stop the whole industry.

The second gate is price and batteries. At about $300,000, the Model A is a toy for the rich today. For flying cars to become normal, factories must build thousands of them, and batteries must become lighter and stronger. The good news is that batteries improve every year. Engineers are counting on that steady progress.

The third gate is the sky itself. If thousands of flying cars fill the air, who controls the traffic? There are no sky lanes, no traffic lights, and no parking lots in the clouds yet. New rules must be written — for safety, for noise, and for privacy. Imagine hundreds of flying cars over your school at lunch time. Somebody has to organize that sky.

Alef is not the only team trying. Other companies are building air taxis — small electric aircraft that also take off straight up. Big airplane makers and rich startups are all in the race. The company that solves safety, price, and rules first will change how cities work.

The flying car is no longer a movie prop. It is a real machine, waiting for rules, better batteries, and time. The question is not whether we will fly to school one day. It is when — and who will build it first.
